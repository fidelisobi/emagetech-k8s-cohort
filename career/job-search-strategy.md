## The 3 Channels That Actually Work

### 1. Recruiter outreach (highest success rate)
Recruiters actively search LinkedIn for K8s keywords.
Optimize your profile (see linkedin-guide.md) and let them come to you.
When they reach out: respond promptly, ask about the tech stack, assess fit.

### 2. Referrals (fastest path to interview)
Someone at the company refers you → your resume goes to the top of the pile.
Ask cohort classmates who are already placed, ask instructors, ask in the Slack.
Don't be shy — referrals are normal and expected in tech.

### 3. Direct applications (lowest success rate but necessary)
Apply to 5–10 roles per week. Use a spreadsheet to track.
Customize the cover message (2–3 sentences) for each application.
Follow up once after 1 week if no response.

## Interview Stages (What to Expect)

```
1. Recruiter screen (30 min phone call)
   → Talk about your background, what you're looking for, salary range
   → Research the company beforehand: what do they build? How do they use K8s?

2. Technical screen (45–60 min)
   → Conceptual questions (use interview-questions.md)
   → Sometimes a take-home: "deploy this app to our cluster" or "debug this YAML"

3. Technical interview (60–90 min)
   → Live coding / debugging on a real or simulated cluster
   → Whiteboard architecture design (how would you build X at scale?)

4. System design (senior roles)
   → "Design a multi-tenant Kubernetes platform for 50 engineering teams"

5. Behavioral / cultural fit
   → STAR answers from your project work
```

## After the Offer

- **Negotiate.** Always. First offer is rarely best offer.
- Equity, remote flexibility, learning budget, and start date are all negotiable.
- Ask: "Is there flexibility on the base?" — even if the answer is no, you lose nothing.
- Get the offer in writing before giving notice anywhere.
