# 💰 Offer & Negotiation Guide

You got an offer. Congratulations — this is the moment everything was for.

Now: **do not accept immediately.** Read this first.

---

## The Golden Rule

**Always negotiate.** Always.

The worst that happens is they say no and the offer stays the same. Companies expect negotiation. A recruiter who rescinds an offer because you asked for more was never a company you wanted to work at.

---

## Step 1 — Evaluate the Full Package

An offer is more than base salary. Evaluate every component:

| Component | What to Ask |
|-----------|------------|
| **Base Salary** | Is this at or above market rate for this role/location? |
| **Equity / RSUs** | What's the vesting schedule? 4-year with 1-year cliff is standard. What's the current valuation? |
| **Signing Bonus** | Is there one? (Always ask if not offered) |
| **Annual Bonus** | What % of salary? What's the target vs actual payout history? |
| **Remote Policy** | Fully remote? Hybrid? How many days in office required? |
| **Health Insurance** | What's covered? Is family covered? What's your out-of-pocket? |
| **401k Match** | Does the company match? Up to what %? When does it vest? |
| **PTO / Vacation** | How many days? Is it use-it-or-lose-it? |
| **Learning Budget** | Is there a training/conference budget? How much? |
| **Start Date** | Can you push it if you need time? |

---

## Step 2 — Know Your Market Rate

Before negotiating, know what the role pays. Sources:

- [kube.careers](https://kube.careers/state-of-kubernetes-jobs-2025-q3) — K8s-specific salary data
- [levels.fyi](https://levels.fyi) — Tech salary data by company and level
- [Glassdoor](https://glassdoor.com) — Company-specific ranges
- [LinkedIn Salary](https://linkedin.com/salary) — Role + location data
- **Ask classmates who are placed** — the most accurate data you'll get

**2025–2026 US Market Ranges (Kubernetes roles):**

| Level | Range |
|-------|-------|
| Junior / Associate | $85k–$115k |
| Mid-level | $115k–$155k |
| Senior | $155k–$200k+ |
| Staff / Principal | $190k–$250k+ |

## Step 3 — The Negotiation Script

When you get the verbal offer:

**Don't say yes immediately.** Say this:
> "Thank you so much — I'm really excited about this opportunity. I'd love to review the full written offer before responding. When can I expect that?"

When you have the written offer, respond with:
> "Thank you for the offer. I'm very excited about joining [Company] and the work the team is doing on [something specific]. Based on my research and the experience I'm bringing — particularly my hands-on work with [relevant project] — I was hoping we could get to $[X]. Is there flexibility there?"

**X** = your target, which should be 10–15% above what they offered.

---

## Step 4 — Negotiating Beyond Salary

If they say the base is fixed, negotiate everything else:

- **Signing bonus:** *"Even if base is fixed, is there room for a signing bonus?"*
- **Extra PTO:** *"Could we add 5 days of additional PTO?"*
- **Remote days:** *"Could I work fully remote with quarterly in-person visits?"*
- **Learning budget:** *"Is there a training budget I could use for CKA certification?"*
- **Earlier equity cliff:** *"Could the cliff be 6 months instead of 12?"*
- **Start date:** *"I can start in 3 weeks instead of 2 — would that work?"* (Or push it if you need it)

---

## Step 5 — Evaluate the Offer Beyond Money

Before signing, ask yourself:

**The role:**
- Will I learn here? Is the tech stack modern?
- Do they use Kubernetes in a way that grows my skills?
- What does the team look like? Who is my manager?

**The company:**
- Is the company growing or contracting?
- How long has the K8s team been established?
- What's the on-call culture like?

**The people:**
- Did the hiring manager seem like someone you'd learn from?
- Did the team seem collaborative or defensive?
- Did you feel respected throughout the process?

A high salary at a toxic company is not a win. Know what you're signing up for.

---

## If You Have Multiple Offers

This is the best position to be in. Use them.

*"I have another offer I'm evaluating. I'd like to join [Company A] — you're my first choice. Can you get to $[X] so I can make this decision easily?"*

This is not a bluff — only say it if it's true. Never fabricate competing offers.

---

## Before You Sign

- [ ] Get the full offer in writing (not just verbal)
- [ ] Verify start date, title, salary, and equity are what was discussed
- [ ] Check that the role is permanent / full-time (not misclassified as contract)
- [ ] If equity: get the strike price and 409A valuation in writing
- [ ] Read the non-compete and IP assignment clauses carefully

---

## Announcing Your Decision

**When accepting:**
> "I'm thrilled to accept. I'll sign and return the offer letter today. Looking forward to joining the team."

**When declining:**
> "After careful consideration, I've decided to accept another opportunity. I have a lot of respect for [Company] and hope our paths cross again."

Always decline gracefully. The tech world is small. The recruiter you decline today may reach out with a better role in 18 months.

---

## Post-Acceptance

- Give appropriate notice at your current role (2 weeks standard, more if you're in a senior role)
- Post your win in the cohort Slack — you'll inspire everyone still in the search
- Tag a TA to update the placement tracker
- Connect with your new team on LinkedIn before day 1
